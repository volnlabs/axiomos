# axiomos v0.3 and v0.4 hardware benchmark campaign

This campaign has one purpose: produce attributable physical evidence for the requested v0.3 and v0.4 acceptance benchmarks on the confirmed Raspberry Pi 5 8 GB and Shrike-Lite V1.0/R0.4 bench. Boot, signing, host tests, firmware fixes, and wiring review are prerequisites only. General performance work, Linux comparisons, OSDI experiments, patent gates, v0.5 scope, and unrelated release closure are outside this plan.

Done means every row in the acceptance matrix has one of three honest outcomes: **pass with retained raw evidence**, **fail with retained raw evidence**, or **blocked with the exact missing prerequisite named**. Source code, simulation, a roadmap statement, or an earlier reconnaissance run never substitutes for a physical result.

<Callout tone="decision">

The benchmark source of truth is the checked-out implementation plus the campaign artifacts. The numerical thresholds copied into this plan are operator-selected acceptance targets, not existing performance claims. The current benchmark authority explicitly says ARM-A and GPIO latency are not yet measured.

</Callout>

<Callout tone="decision">

Locked inputs: use a dedicated lab Ed25519 key; identify the board as Shrike-Lite **V1.0/R0.4**; start the FPGA project from the minimum Vicharak board files for that exact revision; power the Pi with the official 27 W USB-C supply; acquire a regulated, current-limited 6 V motor supply; and use the HC-SR04 interface specified below.

</Callout>

<Callout tone="risk">

Do not flash the repository's current RP2040 binary. Its GPIO0/1 UART, GPIO2 PWM, GPIO4 PWM, and GPIO12 e-stop assignments conflict respectively with the Shrike configuration link, MCU LED, and FPGA power control. The current FPGA RTL also has no V1.0/R0.4 ForgeFPGA project, pin assignment, or physical six-bit-link receiver. These are benchmark-enablement blockers, not validated v0.4 hardware.

</Callout>

<Callout tone="risk">

Pi 5 GPIO IRQ delivery is now implemented against RP1 IO_BANK0 MSI-X vector 0 / GIC interrupt ID 160 and has passed the AArch64 cross-build. It is not yet hardware-proven: a real slow-edge probe must produce `PI5_GPIO_IRQ_PROVEN`, deliver exactly once, clear the RP1 source, and return the parent status inactive without an interrupt storm before any N=10,000 run.

</Callout>

The campaign will freeze `acceptance.toml` before the first measured run. User-supplied requirements are: v0.3 GPIO N=10,000 with a target below 500 ns and a fallback below 1 µs, monitor overhead below 5 µs, exactly 1,000 invalid requests with zero escapes, and e-stop below 1 ms; v0.4 behavior-load reporting with a target below 100 ms, hook and sensor-to-actuator reporting, FPGA e-stop below 1 ms, and a 24-hour zero-panic soak. Statistics and v0.4 sample counts not stated by the user are proposed defaults in the tables below and may change only before `HIL_COMMIT`, never after seeing data.

## Electrical decisions

### HC-SR04 interface

Use a passive echo divider for the first campaign:

```text
Shrike 5 V OUT ---- HC-SR04 VCC
Shrike GND -------- HC-SR04 GND -------- logic analyzer GND

RP2040 TRIG ---- 1.0 kΩ ---- HC-SR04 TRIG

HC-SR04 ECHO ---- 2.2 kΩ ----+---- RP2040 ECHO input
                              |
                             3.3 kΩ
                              |
                             GND
```

At a 5.0 V echo, the input node is `5.0 × 3.3 / (2.2 + 3.3) = 3.0 V`. The RP2040 specifies a 2.0 V minimum high threshold and an `IOVDD + 0.5 V` absolute input limit at 3.3 V I/O, so 3.0 V provides both a valid high and margin below the absolute limit. The HC-SR04 sheet specifies a 5 V supply and a 10 µs TTL trigger. Drive its trigger from 3.3 V through the 1 kΩ resistor; if this particular clone does not reliably recognize 3.3 V, add a 5 V 74AHCT-family buffer for the trigger rather than altering the echo divider.

Before connection, power only the 5 V sensor rail, substitute a steady 5 V input at the divider, and confirm approximately 3.0 V at its output with the multimeter. Never connect HC-SR04 Echo directly to the Shrike. Do not power the sensor from the 6 V motor rail or the L298N module's noisy regulator. The Shrike may supply the sensor from its 5 V header while it is USB-powered only after the R0.4 schematic confirms the header direction; do not simultaneously inject external 5 V into that header.

### E-stop and motor power

The first v0.3 software e-stop uses Pi GPIO24 with an active-low input and no motor attached. Use the same fail-safe NC-contact pattern as the v0.4 gate—normal state held high through the closed contact, press/open state pulled low—then update the stale pull-up/press-to-ground bench comment to match the tested wiring. The Vicharak board itself does not provide the required independent robot e-stop; it is external campaign hardware.

- A latching NC contact connects 3.3 V to the chosen FPGA `estop_n` input; a 10 kΩ pull-down makes a press, broken wire, or disconnected switch assert low.
- A second independent NC contact keeps a normally energized relay or rated power switch enabled. Pressing the e-stop or breaking its cable removes motor power.
- The FPGA output, never an RP2040 bypass, is the only signal connected to each L298N enable input.
- Remove the L298N ENA/ENB high-enable jumpers. Fit and verify a 10 kΩ pull-down at each enable so reset, unpowered, and unconfigured controllers default to motor-disabled.
- Keep Pi power, Shrike logic power, L298N 5 V logic power, and 6 V motor power separately documented and switchable. Join grounds at the reviewed star/reference point; never join unrelated positive rails. Validate that the specific L298N module recognizes 3.3 V FPGA/RP2040 highs and does not back-power either controller when its 5 V logic rail is absent.
- The relay or electronic cut must be DC-rated above the fused combined stall current. Suppress a relay coil with the correct flyback device so release transients do not reset the controllers.

Choose the exact FPGA input/output pins only after the R0.4 schematic and ForgeFPGA I/O planner agree. Record powered-off continuity from each selected header pin to its intended destination.

### Shrike internal link and Pi UART

Vicharak documents GPIO12 as FPGA PWR, GPIO13 as FPGA EN, GPIO0–3 as dual-purpose FPGA configuration/runtime signals, and GPIO14/15 as internally connected to FPGA pins 18/17 by default. Those pins require an explicit configuration-to-runtime ownership handoff.

Use this minimum runtime contract after successful FPGA configuration:

- GPIO2 is SPI clock, GPIO1 is chip-select/frame, GPIO3 carries RP2040-to-FPGA command data, and GPIO0 returns FPGA ready/status. These are the same four configuration-capable lines, reassigned only after the FPGA positively reports ready.
- GPIO14 and GPIO15 carry left and right RP2040 PWM into FPGA pins 18 and 17. They are not used as external header I/O.
- SPI commands are fixed-length, versioned frames containing sequence, signed left/right per-mille commands, flags, and CRC. The FPGA commits both commands atomically only after a complete valid frame; malformed, replayed, stale, or reset state clears command-valid and forces both PWM outputs low.
- A directly wired external `estop_n` input and two selected FPGA header outputs remain outside this six-bit link. Exact pins come from the R0.4 schematic and I/O planner.

The [current Renesas support answer](https://en-support.renesas.com/knowledgeBase/22509228) limits the Workshop HDL flow to Verilog-2005, so the existing `.sv` simulation is not synthesis proof.

For the external Pi link, use this candidate only after the R0.4 schematic and continuity review:

| Pi 5 | Shrike-Lite RP2040 | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| GPIO14 / UART TX | GPIO17 / UART0 RX | 3.3 V signal only |
| GPIO15 / UART RX | GPIO16 / UART0 TX | 3.3 V signal only |
| GND | GND | Connect ground before TX/RX |

Power the Pi from its 27 W supply and Shrike from its own USB-C connection. Do not connect 5 V or 3.3 V rails between them.

## Stage 1 — run and validate v0.3

### Wiring

Shrike remains in factory MicroPython mode and acts only as an external 3.3 V pulse generator on a schematic-reviewed free pin such as GPIO22. Use an in-memory REPL script; do not overwrite its factory `main.py`. Connect that stimulus to Pi GPIO23 (physical pin 16) and analyzer channel 0. Connect Pi GPIO12/PWM0 channel 1 (physical pin 32) to analyzer channel 1. Connect the software e-stop button at Pi GPIO24 (physical pin 18) to analyzer channel 2. Grounds are common; no motor driver or motor is connected.

Configure 24 MHz acquisition and preserve CSV exports with columns `time_s,input_gpio23,pwm_ena_gpio12,estop_gpio24`. First inject one slow edge and require `PI5_GPIO_IRQ_PROVEN`: the correct GIC/RP1 source is identified, one event is delivered, the source is acknowledged, and no interrupt storm follows. Then run 100 slow pulses and confirm one rising input produces exactly one PWM-low response, followed by a clearly separated re-arm. The reducer must reject missing, duplicated, or out-of-order pairs. Then run each accepted campaign at N≥10,000.

At 24 MHz the sample interval is 41.67 ns. Conservatively budget two sample intervals plus recorded clock error/channel skew for an input-to-output interval. `acceptance.toml` must define whether a threshold pass requires the measured statistic plus this uncertainty to remain below the limit. A result whose uncertainty overlaps 500 ns is **indeterminate at this instrument resolution**, not automatically a target pass.

| V03-A | Paired unloaded PWM apply baseline versus the identical path with ARM-A | N≥10,000 pairs | `max(added_ns) < 5,000` | Raw serial counter samples, actual CNTFRQ, reducer output, image hash |

## Stage 3 — unloaded v0.4 integration

Add one boundary at a time:

1. Connect Pi GPIO14/15 UART to Shrike GPIO17/16 as listed above, plus common ground. Keep separate USB power supplies and leave all positive rails unconnected between boards.
2. Capture at least 1,000 valid frames, then inject CRC errors, truncated frames, replayed sequence values, Pi TX silence, Shrike TX silence, and RP2040 reset. Each fault must reach the documented safe/link-down state without a spurious motor command.
3. Connect the HC-SR04 through the validated interface. Capture trigger, divided Echo, Shrike-to-Pi Sensor frames, and IIO hook markers. Confirm at three fixed distances with the multimeter used only for rail verification, not distance timing.
4. Connect RP2040 PWM/runtime-link inputs to the FPGA and the FPGA gated outputs to logic-analyzer channels. Exercise e-stop, commands below/at/above the envelope, stale commands, reset, and link loss. Both outputs must be low whenever any safe condition is active.
5. Connect the L298N logic inputs with the motor supply still off and motors disconnected. Verify direction and enable truth tables at the connector.

**Gate 3:** every fault matrix row passes unloaded, and the logic analyzer proves there is no route around the FPGA enable outputs.

## Stage 4 — controlled motor power

Before applying 6 V, record the supply's model, current limit, polarity, current rating, fuse, wire rating, L298N jumper state, and e-stop relay behavior. Determine motor count and stall current from a trustworthy datasheet or a brief current-limited measurement of one motor at a time. Prefer supply readback or a rated shunt; do not intentionally route stall current through a handheld multimeter unless its fused current range, leads, and expected current are positively within rating. Size the supply, fuse, relay/DC switch, and wiring from combined simultaneous stall current while respecting the L298N's real thermal/current limits.

First power-up order:

1. Wheels raised and mechanically contained; L298N outputs disconnected from motors.
2. With ENA/ENB jumpers removed and 10 kΩ pull-downs fitted, apply Shrike/FPGA logic and confirm both L298N enable nodes remain low through reset and configuration.
3. Apply regulated L298N 5 V logic power, check for controller back-power, then apply 6 V motor power and verify expected input voltage and zero unexpected motor output.
4. Remove motor power, connect one motor, restore power with a low predeclared duty cap, and test start/stop/e-stop.
5. Repeat for the second channel, then both channels. At every step, pressing the physical e-stop must cut motor power and assert the FPGA gate.

An unexpected motion, reset, current-limit event, excessive heating, reversed polarity, PWM while latched, or link-loss motion is a stop condition. Remove motor power and preserve the capture.

**Gate 4:** both channels pass low-duty, direction, watchdog, link-loss, and independent e-stop tests with wheels raised.
